The King of a Thousand Small Tries

Have you ever looked at the stars and wondered... if you have to do something giant to be truly special. In the heart of the Whispering Woods... where the trees have silver bark and the leaves hum a low, green song... there lived a lion cub named Theo. Theo wanted to be a Great King. He spent his mornings watching the older lions. He watched how they could leap across wide, rushing rivers in a single bound. He watched how they could let out a roar so powerful... it made the very clouds tremble in the sky.

Theo would stand on his favorite flat rock... puff out his chest until he felt very round... and try to roar. He would open his mouth wide... take a deep breath of the clover-scented air... and let it out.

Squeak.

It wasn't a roar. It was a tiny, fuzzy sound... like a kitten sneezing in a pile of feathers. Theo would sigh... his shoulders would slump... and he would think that he was just not meant for big, kingly things. He felt like a tiny drop of water in a very, very big ocean.

One afternoon... while the sun was painting long, golden fingers across the forest floor... Theo decided to go for a walk. He felt a bit heavy with sadness. I want to be a hero today... he whispered to a passing dragonfly. But I am too small for big hero things.

He hadn't walked very far when he heard a tiny, frantic sound. It was coming from beneath a large, mossy fern. Theo knelt down... his nose twitching. There... tucked between two damp stones... was a beetle. The beetle was bright blue... like a piece of the sky had fallen down... but it was stuck on its back. Its tiny legs were waving in the air... kicking and pushing... but it couldn't turn over.

Theo looked at the beetle. It was such a small problem. A Great King would probably be busy fighting off giant shadows... or leading a parade... he thought. But Theo reached out one of his big, soft paws. He didn't use his claws. Instead... he used the very tip of his velvet-soft toe to gently... slowly... nudge the beetle.

The beetle tumbled... wiggled... and—pop—it landed on its feet. It paused for a second... waved its antennae at Theo as if to say thank you... and scurried away into the grass.

Theo smiled. That was one small try... he said to himself. It didn't feel like a roar... but it felt good.

A little further down the path... Theo came to the Silver Stream. The water usually sang a happy, bubbling song... but today it sounded choked and grumpy. Theo saw why. A pile of heavy branches and old, wet leaves had drifted together... blocking the path for a family of minnows. The tiny fish were huddled in a small pool... waiting for the water to move again.

Theo looked at the branches. They weren't giant logs. He couldn't move them all at once like his father could. He stood there for a moment... feeling that old, familiar doubt. I cannot clear this stream... he thought. I am just a cub.

But then... he remembered the beetle.

He stepped into the cool, clear water. It felt ticklish between his toes. He reached down and picked up one small stick with his teeth. He carried it to the bank and dropped it. Then... he went back. He picked up one soggy leaf. Then... a small pebble.

He did this ten times. Then twenty. Then fifty. His fur was getting wet... and his jaw was a little tired... but he didn't stop. He moved one small thing at a time. After a while... he moved a medium-sized branch.

Suddenly—whoosh—the water broke through. The stream began to sing its happy song once more. The minnows flicked their silver tails and zoomed away into the current... shimmering like coins in the sun.

That was a lot of small tries... Theo murmured... shaking the water off his paws. He felt a little bit taller.

The sky began to turn a deep, dusty purple. The first stars were beginning to blink awake... like sleepy eyes opening in the dark. As Theo turned to head back to his pride... he heard a soft, weeping sound from high above.

He looked up. Tucked in the branches of a low-hanging willow tree was a tiny bird... a fledgling who had tried to fly too soon. Her wings were shaking... and she was gripping the bark with all her might... too scared to move up or down.

Theo couldn't fly. He couldn't climb as high as the older lions. But he looked at the tree. He saw a low branch. Then a slightly higher one. Then a knot in the wood.

I will try... he whispered.

He jumped to the first branch. His paws slipped a little... but he held on. He didn't look at the top of the tree. He only looked at the next place to put his paw. One jump. One reach. One small try at a time.

He reached the little bird. He didn't roar. He didn't do anything loud. He just sat very still and let the bird hop onto his back... nesting in the soft fur of his neck. Then... very carefully... he climbed back down. He placed the bird in her warm, straw nest on the lower branch where her mother was waiting.

Theo walked back to the lion's camp as the moon rose... a big, silver pearl in the velvet sky. He was tired... and his paws were muddy... and he still hadn't roared once.

When he arrived... his uncle... the Great King... was standing on the high rock. He looked down at Theo and saw the mud... the wet fur... and the scratches on his nose.

Where have you been, Theo... the King asked... his voice like rolling thunder. Have you been practicing your roar? Have you been learning to leap the great rivers?

Theo looked up. He felt small... but he didn't feel sad.

No... Theo said softly. I didn't do anything big today. I just did a thousand small tries.

He told his uncle about the beetle... the minnows... and the tiny bird. He told him how he didn't roar... but he had just kept moving... one small step at a time.

The Great King climbed down from his high rock. He walked over to Theo and rested a heavy... warm paw on the cub's shoulder.

Listen closely, Theo... the King rumbled... and this time... his voice was as soft as a breeze. Many lions can roar. Many can leap. But a true king is not made in one big moment. A king is built from a thousand small acts of kindness... a thousand moments of not giving up... and a thousand small tries when the world feels too big.

The King looked out over the quiet woods.

Today... you didn't roar with your throat, Theo. You roared with your heart. And that is the loudest roar of all.

Theo purred... a low, rhythmic sound that vibrated in his chest. He curled up in the warm grass next to his family. As he closed his eyes... he didn't dream of being a giant... or having a voice of thunder. He dreamed of the beetle... and the stream... and the stars. He fell into a deep... peaceful sleep... knowing that tomorrow was a new day... and he was ready for a thousand more small tries.
