The Cloud Who Could Not Rain

Have you ever looked up at the sky and wondered where the clouds go when they are in a hurry? Most clouds are very busy indeed. They have gardens to water... and rivers to fill... and mountains to wash. But in the far corner of the Great Blue Sky, near the edge where the horizon meets the sea, lived a cloud named Nim. Nim was not a storm cloud. He was not a rain cloud. He was a small... puffy... perfectly white cloud that looked remarkably like a toasted marshmallow.

While the other clouds were dark and heavy, filled with the important business of thunder and lightning, Nim was light as a feather. He spent his days drifting aimlessly, watching the hawks circle and listening to the distant hum of the world below. But Nim was not happy. He wanted to be useful. He wanted to hear the pitter-patter of his own droplets hitting the leaves of the forest. He wanted to be part of the Great Water Cycle that the elder clouds talked about with such pride.

One afternoon, a massive cumulonimbus cloud named Barnaby—who was as wide as a city and as grey as an old wool coat—drifted past Nim. Barnaby was grumbling. His belly was full of rain, and every few minutes, he would let out a low, vibrating growl of thunder.

Move aside, little puff, Barnaby rumbled. I have a drought to break in the Northern Valley. I have work to do! Important work!

Nim scrunched himself up. He tried to turn grey. He held his breath until his edges felt tight, trying to squeeze out even a single, tiny drop of water. But no matter how hard he tried... he remained as white as a fresh sheet of paper. He was simply too light. He was too airy.

I am just a decoration, Nim sighed. His voice was like a soft breeze through a willow tree. The sky would be just as blue without me.

Discouraged, Nim let the high-altitude winds take him. He drifted away from the busy storm tracks and the crowded mountain peaks. He floated over the Great Desert, where the sand looked like ripples of gold... and over the Deep Ocean, where the whales sang songs that made his misty heart ache with beauty.

Eventually, he found himself drifting over a small, dusty valley. It was a place the big rain clouds often missed. The grass was the color of old straw, and the flowers were bowing their heads, their petals curled and dry. In the middle of this valley was a tiny stone cottage with a very small garden.

Nim looked down and saw an old gardener. The man was wearing a wide-brimmed hat, and he was kneeling in the dirt, trying to clear away the weeds around a single, wilting rosebush. The sun was high and fierce, beating down with a relentless, golden heat. The gardener wiped his forehead with a handkerchief and let out a long, tired sigh.

It is too hot today, the gardener whispered. If only I could have a moment of peace from this sun... just a moment to breathe.

Nim looked at the man. He looked at the dry, cracked earth. He knew he could not rain. He did not have a single drop of water to give to the thirsty roses. He felt a deep, heavy sadness. But then... he noticed something. When he floated directly between the sun and the gardener... a cool, dark shape appeared on the ground.

A shadow.

Nim stopped. He spread himself out as wide as he could. He fluffed up his edges and stretched his misty arms until he was no longer a small marshmallow, but a wide, thin blanket of white. He hovered perfectly still, right above the gardener and his tiny stone cottage.

Below, the gardener gasped. He took off his hat and looked up, his eyes widening with surprise. The fierce heat of the sun vanished. In its place was a soft, cool dimness. The temperature in the garden dropped... and for the first time in hours, a gentle breeze began to stir the leaves.

Oh... thank you, little cloud, the gardener breathed. He sat back on his heels and closed his eyes, soaking in the coolness. This is exactly what I needed.

Nim felt a strange, tingly sensation. It wasn't the heavy weight of rain... it was something else. It was the feeling of being exactly where he was supposed to be. He watched as the gardener, refreshed by the shade, found the energy to walk to the well and draw a bucket of cool water. The man carried the water to the rosebush and poured it carefully over the roots.

The roses didn't get rain from Nim... but they got the water because Nim had given the gardener the strength to fetch it.

Nim stayed there all afternoon. He didn't move an inch. He became a parasol for the valley. He watched as the birds came out from the trees, no longer afraid of the burning sun. He watched as a small cat stretched out on the porch of the cottage, enjoying the cool tiles.

He realized that the big clouds like Barnaby were like the heavy buckets of water—essential and powerful—but he... he was the quiet hand that held the bucket. He was the relief. He was the pause in the middle of a hot day.

As the sun began to set, turning the sky into a sea of fire and gold, Nim felt himself glowing. Because he was so light and so high, he caught the very last rays of light, turning a brilliant, vibrant pink... then a deep, royal purple.

The gardener came out of his cottage one last time before bed. He looked up at the glowing cloud and waved.

Goodnight, my friend, the man called out. Thank you for the shade.

Nim felt a wave of peace wash over him. He wasn't a storm. He wasn't a flood. He was Nim... the cloud who brought the cool. He let the night wind pick him up again, but this time, he didn't feel useless. He felt like a guardian.

He drifted over the sleeping valley, watching the silver stars begin to pop through the velvet dark. He listened to the crickets start their nighttime symphony—chirp... pause... chirp. He felt the coolness of the night air settle into his misty folds.

I am the shade, Nim whispered to the stars. And the shade is a gift too.

And there, high above the silent world, the little cloud who found his purpose tucked himself into a pocket of still air and fell into a soft... light... and perfectly content sleep.
