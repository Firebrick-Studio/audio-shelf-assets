The Serpent’s Mercy

Have you ever felt the weight of a gaze you cannot see... the heavy... silent presence of something ancient and powerful watching from the tall grass? In the forest of Chandan-van... where the trees are so old their roots have turned to stone... there lived a Great Cobra named Vasuki.

Vasuki was a creature of terrifying beauty. His scales were the color of a moonless midnight... and when he moved... he did not crawl so much as flow... like ink spilled across a silk carpet. For years... he was the undisputed master of the forest edge. The woodcutters would not enter his shadow... and the village children were told stories of his lightning-fast strike to keep them from wandering too far into the deep brush. Vasuki lived in a state of absolute... undisturbed peace. Because he was feared... he was respected.

But one afternoon... as the heat of the day began to melt into a soft... amber glow... a wandering monk sat down beneath Vasuki’s banyan tree. The monk carried nothing but a wooden bowl and a heart full of stillness. When Vasuki slid from his hollow... hood flared... ready to deliver his deadly warning... the monk did not flinch. He simply looked at the serpent with eyes that were as calm as a mountain lake.

Friend... the monk said... his voice like the low hum of a distant bell... why do you live your life in a state of war? You carry enough poison to end a dozen lives... yet you have no peace of your own. True strength does not need to bite.

Vasuki was fascinated. He had never met a creature that did not tremble. He listened as the monk spoke of the beauty of non-violence... of the path of mercy... and the merit of causing no harm to any living soul. By the time the moon rose... Vasuki had made a solemn vow. He promised the monk that he would never strike another creature. He retracted his fangs... he tucked away his venom... and he retreated into his hollow... determined to become a being of pure... silent light.

But the world... as the Great Sage Chanakya once observed... is not always ready for a saint.

When the village children realized the Great Cobra no longer struck at them... their fear turned to curiosity... and then... to cruelty. They began to throw sharp stones into his hollow to watch him squirm. The woodcutters... seeing him so passive... began to use his tail to tie their bundles of sticks... laughing as the serpent hissed in pain but refused to bite. The cattle-herders would step on him with their heavy boots... and the once-mighty Vasuki became a broken... battered thing... hiding in the dark... wet mud... his beautiful scales torn and dull.

A year later... the monk returned to the forest. He found Vasuki thin and bleeding... curled into a tight... miserable knot beneath the roots of the banyan tree.

My friend... the monk said with great sadness... what has happened to you? Why are you in such a state of ruin?

Vasuki looked up... his eyes cloudy with pain. I followed your path... Master. I gave up my bite. I gave up my anger. And in return... the world has crushed me. To be merciful is to be a carpet for the feet of the arrogant. Your teaching has been my undoing.

The monk sighed... a long... weary sound that seemed to pull the very leaves from the trees. He sat down and reached out a hand... stopping just short of the serpent's bruised head.

I told you not to bite... Vasuki... the monk whispered... his voice firm yet gentle. I told you not to cause harm. But... I never told you not to hiss.

Vasuki went still.

You confused mercy with weakness... the monk continued. You surrendered your fangs... but you also surrendered your dignity. To live in peace in a world of thorns... you must maintain the aura of your power. You do not have to be poisonous to be respected... but you must show the world that you could be... if the need arose. The hiss is your boundary. The hiss is the wall that protects your sanctuary.

The next morning... a group of boys came to the banyan tree... carrying heavy sticks to poke at the "cowardly snake." As they reached into the hollow... Vasuki did not strike. He did not sink his fangs into their skin.

Instead... he slid out from the roots with a speed they had forgotten. He reared up until he was as tall as the youngest boy’s chest... and he spread his magnificent... obsidian hood until it blocked out the sun. He let out a long... thunderous hiss—a sound like the roar of a hidden fire. It was a sound that shook the very air in their lungs.

The boys dropped their sticks and fled... screaming in a terror that they would never forget. The woodcutters stopped their mocking. The herders walked a wide... respectful circle around the tree.

Vasuki returned to his hollow. He was still a creature of peace. He still held no malice in his heart... and his fangs remained unused. But he kept his hood ready. He kept his hiss strong. And in that shadow—in the formidable aura of his own potential power—he finally found the peace he had been seeking.

As you settle into your blankets now... think about your own hiss. It is not anger. It is not a desire to hurt others or to seek revenge. It is simply your boundary. It is the invisible line that tells the world... here is my peace... and it is sacred.

You do not need to fight the world to be safe. You do not need to be "poisonous" to survive. You only need to stand in the truth of your own strength. You only need to let the world know that your kindness is a choice... and not a lack of options.

Feel the strength in your own breath now. Feel the calm... steady power in your limbs as they grow heavy and relaxed. You are the guardian of your own quiet forest. Spread your hood... settle into the safety of your own boundaries... and let the noise and the stones of the world stay on the other side of the line.

The forest is quiet. The banyan tree is still. And you... are perfectly protected. You are both merciful... and formidable.
