The Mountain That Wanted to Move

Deep in the center of the Great Continent... where the earth rises up to meet the stars in a jagged line of silver and grey... lived a mountain named Old Granite. Old Granite was very... very old. He had been standing in the same spot for millions of years. He had seen the first forests grow like a green carpet at his feet... and he had felt the first rivers carve deep... icy paths through his stone. He was massive... and strong... and completely... perfectly still.

But Old Granite had a secret. He was tired of being a mountain.

Every day... he watched the world move around him. He watched the Great Clouds sail across the sky like giant... white ships... traveling from the frozen North to the tropical South. He watched the rivers dash and giggle over the rocks... always in a hurry to find the sea. He watched the eagles soar on the invisible currents of the air... seeing things he could only imagine.

I am a stone... Granite sighed. His voice was a low... rumbling sound that felt like a tiny earthquake in the valley below. I have seen the same three lakes and the same pine forest for a thousand lifetimes. I want to see the Great Blue Ocean. I want to feel the sand between my toes... if I had toes. I want to move.

One afternoon... the North Wind came whistling through the high passes. The Wind was a traveler... a wild... restless spirit who smelled of salt spray and arctic ice. He spun in circles around Granite’s snowy peak... making the small pebbles dance.

North Wind... Granite rumbled... catching a gust in a deep... stone bowl near his summit. You have been everywhere. You have seen the whales breach in the deep water... and you have seen the cities made of glass and gold. Tell me... how can a mountain learn to walk?

The Wind stopped spinning and hovered... a cool... invisible presence. To walk is to leave things behind... Granite. Why would a mountain... who is the foundation of the world... want to leave?

Because I am bored... Granite replied. I am just a pile of rocks. I want to be a traveler... like you.

The North Wind let out a long... whistling sigh. Very well... Granite. I cannot move you. Only the Earth can do that... and she moves very... very slowly. But I can show you what you are seeing without ever moving an inch. Close your eyes.

Granite closed his heavy... stone eyelids. He felt the Wind press against his eastern face.

Listen... the Wind whispered. Do not listen to the silence. Listen to the life.

Granite focused. He stopped thinking about the distant ocean and started feeling his own skin. High up near his peak... he felt the tiny... scratching claws of a mountain goat. The goat was balanced on a ledge no wider than a dinner plate... safe from the wolves because Granite provided a high... sturdy sanctuary. He felt the warmth of the goat’s breath against his cold stone.

Move your mind lower... the Wind commanded.

Granite felt his waist... where the Great Pine Forest grew. He felt the roots of a thousand trees reaching deep into his cracks... like tiny... wooden fingers. They weren't just taking from him—they were holding him together. He felt a family of bears sleeping in a cave on his northern slope... their slow... steady heartbeats thrumming against his granite heart. Thump... thump... thump.

He felt the Silver Stream. It wasn't just water running away from him. It was his own snow... melting in the sun... giving itself to the valley so the flowers could bloom and the deer could drink. He wasn't a wall... he was a fountain.

Now... the Wind whispered... feel the travelers.

Far down... near his base... a group of hikers was slowly making their way along a winding path. They were tired. Their packs were heavy... and their boots were covered in dust. But as they reached a high ridge... they stopped. Granite felt their collective gasp of wonder.

Look... a young girl whispered. Her voice carried up the slope on a thermal. You can see the whole world from here. The mountain is so big... it makes everything else feel... peaceful.

She sat down on a flat... sun-warmed rock. She patted the stone with her hand. Thank you... Mountain... she said softly. Thank you for staying still for us.

Granite felt a strange... warm sensation... like the first ray of spring sun hitting a glacier. He realized that if he had moved... if he had gone to find the ocean... the goats would have no home. The bears would have no cave. The trees would have no place to put their roots... and the girl would have no place to see the world.

He wasn't a prisoner of the earth. He was its protector.

The North Wind began to pick up speed again... preparing to fly over the Great Desert. Do you still want to leave... Old Granite?

The mountain let out a long... deep breath of mist. The clouds around his head swirled and parted... revealing the valley below in all its vibrant... green glory.

No... Granite rumbled. His voice was no longer a grumble... it was a song of deep... resonant stone. I do not need to move to see the world. The world is right here... and I am holding it up.

The North Wind laughed... a sound like chimes in the distance. You are a wise mountain... Granite. You are the stillness that allows the rest of us to move.

Granite settled back into his ancient foundation. He didn't feel bored anymore. He felt a deep sense of purpose. He watched the clouds sail by... and he wasn't jealous. He knew they were coming to him to rest. He watched the eagles fly... and he knew they were using his height to touch the sun.

As the afternoon light began to turn to a soft... hazy purple... Granite felt a quiet strength spreading through his peaks. He smelled the scent of the pines and the cold... clean air of the heights. He listened to the steady... rhythmic thrum of the earth beneath him... and the tiny... happy sounds of the creatures living on his back.

He was Old Granite. He was the Mountain. And as the stars began to appear one by one... hanging like lanterns above his snowy head... he stood tall and proud... the quietest... strongest... and most contented traveler in the whole world.

The wind died down to a whisper... the valley grew dim... and the mountain remained... a silent guardian over a world that was... for a moment... perfectly at peace.
