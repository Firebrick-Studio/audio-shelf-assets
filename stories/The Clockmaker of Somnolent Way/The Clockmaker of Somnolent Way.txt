The Clockmaker of Somnolent Way

Have you ever noticed how the hands of a clock seem to chase each other... as if they are in a race that never ends? In the bustling town of Hurry-Up-Hills... the people were always looking at their wrists. They lived by the tick... and they lived by the tock... always worrying about where they needed to be next. They ate their breakfast while walking to the carriage... and they read their papers while drinking their tea... and they always... always felt like they were running out of time.

But tucked away at the very end of a winding... cobblestone street called Somnolent Way... was a shop that looked like it had been built from a dream. The window was filled with gears that turned as slowly as a honey jar... and the door was made of dark... polished mahogany that smelled like cedar and ancient secrets. This was the shop of Master Thomas... the Clockmaker.

Master Thomas was a man who moved like a calm river. His hair was the color of wood ash... and his eyes were as clear and steady as a mountain pool. While the rest of the town was busy checking their pocket watches... Master Thomas spent his days listening. He listened to the rhythm of the world—the soft patter of rain on the roof... the rhythmic breathing of his old tabby cat... and the gentle hum of the earth beneath the floorboards.

One afternoon... the Mayor of Hurry-Up-Hills marched into the shop. The Mayor was a man who wore three watches at once... and his face was the color of a very ripe tomato.

Master Thomas! the Mayor shouted... his voice echoing off the silent walls. My clocks are too slow! My citizens are missing their meetings! The town is falling behind schedule! I need you to build us a Great Clock for the square... one with a tick so loud and a tock so sharp that no one will ever dare to be late again!

Master Thomas looked at the Mayor. He didn't rush his answer. He picked up a tiny... silver gear and polished it with a soft cloth.

A clock that shouts is a clock that steals... Master Thomas said softly. Time is not a master to be feared... Mr. Mayor. It is a gift to be tasted.

Nonsense! the Mayor barked. Build the clock! Build it fast!

Master Thomas nodded slowly. He went to work. But he did not build a clock with a loud tick. He did not build a clock with a sharp tock. Instead... he gathered sounds. He went to the forest and recorded the whisper of the wind through the willow trees. He went to the stream and captured the sound of water gurgling over smooth stones. He even sat with his cat for three days... recording the deep... steady purr of a contented heart.

A month later... the Great Clock was revealed in the town square. A massive crowd had gathered... everyone checking their watches... everyone tapping their feet in a hurry. The Mayor pulled the velvet rope... and the town held its breath.

The hands of the clock were long and elegant... made of polished brass. But as the first minute passed... there was no tick. Instead... a sound like a soft... summer rain began to drift from the tower.

Pitter... patter... pitter... patter.

The people stopped tapping their feet. They looked at each other... confused.

Where is the tick? a merchant cried. How do I know the time is passing?

But then... the clock let out a long—peaceful—sigh... and the sound changed. Now... it sounded like the distant... rhythmic crashing of waves on a sandy beach. Whoosh... shhh... whoosh... shhh.

The people began to breathe more deeply. Their shoulders... which had been hunched up toward their ears... began to drop. The Mayor... who was reaching for his three watches... suddenly stopped. He realized he was smelling the scent of the nearby bakery for the first time in years. It smelled of yeast... and sugar... and warmth.

The Great Clock didn't remind them of what they were missing. It reminded them of where they were.

Master Thomas stood in the shadow of the tower... watching. A young girl walked up to him... holding her father’s hand. The father wasn't pulling her along this time. He was standing still... watching a butterfly land on a nearby flower.

Why doesn't it go tick-tock? the girl asked.

Because... Master Thomas whispered... time doesn't happen in pieces. It happens all at once. Like a song. When you listen to the tick... you are only waiting for the tock. You are living for the next second... and you are missing this one.

The town of Hurry-Up-Hills began to change. They didn't stop working... and they didn't stop meeting... but they stopped rushing. They realized that a minute spent listening to the rain-clock was a minute that felt like an hour of peace. They began to sit on their porches. They began to talk to their neighbors. They began to realize that the most important time was the time they were in... right now.

Eventually... the Mayor took off two of his watches and gave them away. He kept only one... and he had Master Thomas replace the ticking heart with the sound of a small... chirping cricket.

Somnolent Way became the most popular street in the town. People would walk there just to feel the quiet. Master Thomas’s shop remained... a place of gears and dreams... but he never made another ticking clock.

Whenever someone asked him what time it was... he would smile... look at the light filtering through the trees... and say the same thing.

It is the perfect time... he would say. It is exactly now.

And as the sun reached its highest point... casting a warm... golden glow over the square... the people of the town felt a deep... rhythmic calm. They moved with a steady grace... listening to the beautiful... wordless song of the world.

The rain-clock played on... a soft... steady promise that there was always enough time... if only you were quiet enough to hear it.
