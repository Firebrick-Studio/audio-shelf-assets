Oliver and the Great Savanna Race

The morning air over the savanna was crisp and cool... tasting faintly of fresh dew and dry grass. It was a special day. You could feel the excitement vibrating in the ground... a low, eager hum that traveled through the roots of the acacia trees. Today was the day of the Great Savanna Race.

Every animal, big and small, had gathered at the starting line near the watering hole. There were sleek cheetahs... stretching their long legs. There were bouncy gazelles... springing into the air like they had springs in their hooves. There were noisy ostriches... fluffing their feathers and looking very serious indeed.

And then... there was Oliver.

Oliver was a young lion with paws that were just a little too big for the rest of him... and a mane that was more fuzz than fur. He stood near the back of the crowd. His stomach felt like it was full of fluttering moths. Thump... thump... thump... went his heart against his ribs.

You see... Oliver was not fast. When he tried to run quickly... his big paws sometimes got tangled up... and he would end up rolling in the dust like a furry ball. He looked at the cheetahs near the front. They looked like coiled springs... ready to snap into action.

I will never keep up... Oliver whispered to himself. His ears drooped low. He thought about slipping away... back to the comfy shade of his favorite sleeping rock.

But then... a deep, rumbling voice boomed across the plains. It was Barnaby the Elephant... the oldest and wisest judge of the race.

Runners... take your marks... Barnaby bellowed... his trunk raised high in the air.

Oliver took a deep breath. He dug his claws into the cool earth.

Get set...

Oliver squeezed his eyes shut.

GO!

The sound was like thunder. The ground shook as hundreds of paws... hooves... and claws pushed off at the exact same moment. A giant cloud of golden dust rose up... smelling of dry earth and sunshine.

Oliver coughed and blinked. By the time the dust cleared a little... all he could see were the tails of the other animals disappearing over the first hill. The zebras were a blur of black and white stripes. The cheetahs were just streaks of spotted lightning.

Even the warthogs... with their short legs and trotting run... were far ahead of him.

Oliver started to run. His gait was a bit rocking... a sort of lolloping gallop. Lollop... lollop... puff... puff.

The first part of the race went through the tall, whispering grass. It brushed against Oliver’s sides... tickling his nose. He could hear the distant cheers of the monkeys sitting in the trees ahead... cheering for the leaders.

He tried to run faster. He pushed harder with his back legs. But his big front paws just seemed to get heavier. He tripped over a hidden root and stumbled... sliding on his belly in the dirt.

Oliver lay there for a moment. The dust settled on his fuzzy mane. The silence around him felt very big. He was already last. Why was he even trying? It would be so easy to just stay here... in the soft grass... and take a nap.

Just then... he heard a slow, scratching sound nearby. Scritch... scratch... scritch... scratch.

Oliver lifted his head. Moving across the path... very, very slowly... was Tilda the Tortoise. She was carrying a single, bright red hibiscus flower in her mouth.

Good morning, young Oliver... Tilda said... her voice sounding like dry leaves rustling together. A bit of a tumble, eh?

Oliver sighed. I am last, Tilda. Everyone else is miles away. I am not built for racing.

Tilda stopped and slowly blinked her wrinkled eyes. She dropped the flower gently. The race is not always to the swift... she whispered. The path to the finish line is the same length for everyone. The only way to fail... is to stop walking.

She picked up her flower and continued her slow... steady journey across the path. Scritch... scratch.

Oliver watched her go. She wasn't worrying about the cheetahs. She was just worrying about her next step.

Oliver stood up. He shook the dust off his coat. He looked at the horizon. The finish line was the Great Baobab Tree... a giant tree that looked like it was planted upside down... way in the distance.

It looked very far away.

I will just run to that big gray rock up ahead... Oliver decided. Just to the rock.

He started lolloping again. Lollop... lollop. He made it to the rock.

Okay... he huffed. Now... just to that crooked acacia tree.

The sun climbed higher in the sky. It turned from a gentle morning yellow to a fierce midday white. The heat shimmered off the ground... making the air look wobbly. Oliver was hot. His tongue was hanging out. His legs felt like tree trunks.

He passed a group of meerkats standing on their termite mound sentry posts. They weren't cheering anymore. They just watched him go by... their heads turning slowly as he passed.

He felt embarrassed to be so slow. But then he remembered Tilda. The only way to fail is to stop walking.

One paw... then the other paw. Breathe in... breathe out.

He ran through the rocky canyon... where his echoes bounced back at him... making him sound like a whole herd of lions. He ran across the dried riverbed... where the cracked mud felt strange under his pads.

Hours passed. The bright white sun began to sink lower... turning golden... then orange... then a deep, bruise-like purple.

The savanna grew quiet. The crickets began their evening song. Chirp... chirp... chirp.

Oliver was so tired. Every muscle in his body wanted to sit down. But up ahead... silhouetted against the purple sky... was the Great Baobab Tree.

He could see the finish line banner... drooping a little in the evening breeze. There was no crowd. The cheetahs and zebras were long gone... probably already asleep after their victory feasts.

But sitting right under the tree... was a large, dark shape.

It was Barnaby the Elephant.

Oliver mustered the very last bit of energy he had. He broke into a wobbly trot. He crossed the finish line and collapsed onto the cool grass right at Barnaby's giant feet.

His chest heaved up and down. Huff... puff... huff... puff.

Barnaby reached down with his trunk and gently patted Oliver on the head. The trunk felt leathery and warm.

Well done, young Oliver... Barnaby rumbled deeply.

Oliver looked up... his eyes stinging with sweat and dust. But I am last, Barnaby. The race was over hours ago.

Barnaby chuckled... a sound that felt like a gentle earthquake.

The sun has set... yes... Barnaby agreed. But the race is only over when you decide it is over. Many animals started today. Some ran fast. Some got tired and stopped in the shade. But you, Oliver... you kept going when it was hot. You kept going when you were alone. You kept going when it felt impossible.

Barnaby leaned in closer.

To be fast is a gift of the legs... Barnaby whispered. But to finish what you start... that is a gift of the heart. And that, little lion... is the greatest victory of all.

Oliver lay there under the ancient tree... listening to the wise elephant and the crickets singing their lullabies. He was sore. He was tired. He was last.

But as he drifted off to sleep right there on the finish line... Oliver didn't feel like a loser. He felt strong. He had started... and he had finished. And he knew that tomorrow... whatever new race lay ahead... he would have the heart to run it... one brave step at a time.
