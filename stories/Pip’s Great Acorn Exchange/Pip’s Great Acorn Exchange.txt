Pip’s Great Acorn Exchange.

Do you know the sound of a secret being kept... deep in the roots of an ancient tree? It is a quiet... rustling sound... like the earth itself is breathing. In the Great Oak Woods... where the air always smells of crushed pine needles and damp moss... lived a tiny mouse named Pip. Pip was no larger than a thumb... with fur the color of toasted oats and ears that were as soft as rose petals.

While the other mice were busy hoarding mountains of grain for themselves... Pip was a collector of curiosities. He didn't care for having the most... he cared for having the best stories.

One crisp... golden afternoon... Pip was scouting near the edge of the Sunlit Clearing when he found it. It was the Great Acorn. It was perfectly round... polished to a high... mahogany shine... and its cap looked like a tiny... hand-carved crown. It was the kind of acorn that squirrels would dream about for years. Pip’s heart did a little dance—thump... thump... thump. This acorn was a treasure. It was enough to feed him for a whole month.

He began to roll it home. He pushed with his tiny shoulders—heave... grunt... heave—and the acorn rolled slowly over the mossy ground.

But as he reached the Twisted Root... he heard a sound. It was a low... shaky sob. Pip stopped. He peered around the root and saw Silas the Squirrel. Silas was usually the fastest climber in the woods... but today... his tail was drooping and his eyes were red.

What is wrong... Silas? Pip asked... his voice high and gentle.

Silas sniffed. I was so busy playing in the leaves that I forgot to watch my stash. A thieving magpie took everything I had saved for my family. Now... I have nothing to give them.

Pip looked at his Great Acorn. It was so shiny. It was so perfect. He felt the weight of it against his paws. Then... he looked at Silas’s sad face.

Pip didn't hesitate. He gave the acorn a final... strong shove. Here... Silas. Take this. It is the finest acorn in the woods. Your family will love it.

Silas gasped. He looked at the acorn... then at the tiny mouse. You would give this to me? But it is a king’s treasure!

Pip smiled... and his whiskers twitched. It is just an acorn... Silas. But a full belly is a better treasure.

Silas was so grateful that he reached into his pocket and pulled out a piece of smooth... sea-green glass he had found by the stream. I cannot take it for free... Pip. Please... take this. It is a piece of the fallen sky.

Pip took the glass. It was cool and beautiful... and when he looked through it... the whole world turned the color of a deep... underwater forest.

He continued on his way... carrying his piece of the sky. But soon... he came across Hattie the Hedgehog. Hattie was trying to build a new window for her winter burrow... but the mud she was using kept crumbling. She looked tired and frustrated.

Pip looked at his green glass. He saw how it caught the light. He knew it would make a perfect... beautiful window for Hattie.

Hattie... Pip called out. Look at this. It will stay strong against the wind... and it will make your home feel like a garden.

He handed her the glass. Hattie’s eyes lit up. Oh... Pip! It is wonderful! I can see the forest even when I am inside! She reached into her nest and pulled out a bundle of dried... fragrant lavender... tied with a blade of grass. Take this... Pip. It is the scent of a summer evening.

Pip took the lavender. It smelled so sweet it made his head feel light and happy. He walked on... smelling his bouquet... until he saw Beatrice the Bluebird sitting on a low branch. Beatrice looked cold. Her feathers were ruffled... and she was shivering in the autumn breeze.

Pip didn't need to think. He climbed up the branch... which was a very long way for a small mouse... and laid the lavender at her feet. This will keep you warm... Beatrice. And the smell will remind you of the sun until the spring returns.

Beatrice tucked the lavender under her wing. The shivering stopped almost at once. Thank you... Pip. You have a very kind soul. She reached down with her beak and plucked a single... brilliant... sky-blue feather from her wing. Take this... Pip. It is a piece of the wind.

Pip carried the blue feather. It was so light he hardly felt it. He was almost home now... but near his front door... he saw a family of tiny shrews. They were huddled together in the tall grass... looking lost and cold. The smallest baby shrew was crying.

Pip looked at his blue feather. It was the last thing he had. If he gave it away... he would have nothing to show for his long day of work. He would have no acorn... no glass... and no lavender.

But then... he saw the baby shrew’s shivering paws. Pip knelt down and wrapped the blue feather around the little shrew like a soft... warm blanket.

There you go... Pip whispered. A piece of the wind to keep the cold away.

The shrews thanked him... their voices like tiny bells. Pip walked into his empty house. He sat down on his mossy bed. He looked at his empty shelves. He had started the day with a treasure... and now... he had nothing at all.

But as he sat there... something strange happened. He didn't feel poor. He felt... heavy. But it was a good weight. It was the weight of a dozen smiles. It was the weight of a squirrel's thanks... and a hedgehog's joy... and a bird's warmth.

Suddenly... there was a knock at his door.

Pip opened it and stared. Standing there were Silas... and Hattie... and Beatrice... and the shrew family. And they weren't empty-handed. Silas brought a handful of sweet hazelnuts. Hattie brought a pile of soft... dry leaves for his bed. Beatrice brought a string of red berries... and the shrews brought a thimble full of clear... sweet honey.

We realized... Silas said... that you spent your whole day taking care of us. So... we decided to take care of you.

They all came inside Pip’s small house. They shared the nuts and the honey. They told stories and laughed as the stars began to peek through the trees. Pip realized that his house wasn't empty anymore. It was full. It was fuller than it had ever been when he was just collecting things for himself.

He had traded one acorn for a whole forest of friends.

As the evening deepened and the woods grew still... Pip sat in the middle of his warm... leaf-lined bed. He listened to the quiet breathing of the woods. He smelled the lingering scent of the lavender on his fur. He felt a deep... golden glow in his chest... a warmth that had nothing to do with the fire and everything to do with the love of his neighbors.

The world was big... and he was small... but Pip felt like the richest mouse in the whole wide world.
